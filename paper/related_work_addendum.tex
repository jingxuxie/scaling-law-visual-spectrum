\paragraph{Solvable and dynamical scaling-law models.}
Beyond empirical curve fitting, several works derive scaling behavior from spectral or random-feature models.  \citet{maloney2022solvable} construct a solvable joint data--random-feature model in which power-law data statistics translate into parameter and sample scaling.  \citet{bordelon2024dynamical} analyze training-time, model-size, and data-size scaling in a dynamical random-feature model, while \citet{zhang2024field} extend solvable random-feature scaling beyond the ridgeless limit.  The sharp one-pass analysis of \citet{lin2024scaling} and its data-reuse extension \cite{lin2025datareuse} isolate approximation, optimization, and effective-dimension terms.  Our work is complementary: rather than fixing gradient descent, we ask how the optimizer changes the effective spectrum entering those decompositions.

\paragraph{Optimizer scaling and hyperparameter transfer.}
A related line asks how optimizer advantages persist with model size and whether hyperparameters transfer across scales.  The $\mu$P framework enables zero-shot hyperparameter transfer by choosing a parameterization with stable update statistics \cite{yang2022tensor}; subsequent theory studies width, depth, data, and transfer in deep linear networks \cite{bordelon2025deep}.  Recent work reports that matrix-preconditioned optimizers require carefully scaled learning rates, blocking, normalization, and weight decay to maintain gains across model sizes \cite{qiu2025hyperparameter}.  Contemporaneous empirical studies directly fit optimizer-dependent scaling laws in random-feature, quadratic, and representation-spectrum settings \cite{ramani2026optimizer,volkova2026robust,jha2026spectral}.  These works motivate optimizer-aware forecasting.  Our distinct contribution is a sufficient-and-negative theory for coordinatewise adaptation: the exponent changes precisely through the spectrum visible to second moments, and can disappear under a basis change even when the underlying eigenvalues are unchanged.

\paragraph{Spectra of random-feature maps.}
Random-feature theory has studied statistical approximation, spectral concentration, and exact high-dimensional limits \cite{avron2017random,liao2018spectrum,ma2020slow,chen2022concentration,bosch2023precise}.  These results characterize when finite feature maps approximate kernels or when their Gram spectra concentrate.  We focus on a different but related object: the diagonal of the feature covariance in the \emph{optimizer coordinates}.  Two feature maps with comparable eigenvalue spectra can expose very different diagonals and therefore induce different coordinatewise adaptive preconditioners.  The spectral oracle controls in our experiments separate feature-map information from the information actually accessible to a diagonal optimizer.
